Model Narrative Response:

The Central Limit Theorem describes a specific process: We start by repeatedly taking random samples and calculating the average (mean) for each one. We then plot these averages on a graph. Even though the original population of annual household income is skewed, the distribution of these sample means becomes Normal. This happens because the act of averaging dilutes the extreme outliers found in the skewed population. As we add more averages to our plot, the resulting graph of sample means forms a Normal distribution (Bell Curve).

3. Worked Solution (Trig)

Step/Work table for autograded questions. No background fill, no checkboxes. Each step includes an explanation of what to do and why. Green answer highlight in last row.

$solutionguide (trig)
▸▾ Step-by-Step Solution
Step Work
Given The point P = (−√3/2, 1/2) is on the unit circle. We need to find the reference angle in both degrees and radians.
Step 1 Identify the angle and its quadrant.
Look up the coordinates on the unit circle. The x-coordinate is negative and the y-coordinate is positive, which places this point in Quadrant II. The standard angle with these coordinates is 150°.
Step 2 Apply the reference angle rule.
The reference angle is the acute angle between the terminal side and the x-axis. Each quadrant has its own formula:
  • Quadrant I: reference angle = θ
  • Quadrant II: reference angle = 180 − θ
  • Quadrant III: reference angle = θ − 180
  • Quadrant IV: reference angle = 360 − θ
Since 150° is in Quadrant II, we use 180 − θ:
Reference angle = 180 − 150 = 30°
Step 3 Convert degrees to radians.
To convert any angle from degrees to radians, multiply by π/180:
30 × π/180 = π/6
Reference angle: 30° = π/6 radians
